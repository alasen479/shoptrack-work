How to Manage a Business With Multiple Employees in Cameroon

Adding staff is supposed to help your business grow. But without the right systems, more employees means more risk.

Hiring your first employee is a milestone. Hiring your second, third, and fourth is when things get complicated. What seemed manageable when it was just you becomes significantly harder when multiple people are handling transactions, serving customers, and making decisions on your behalf.

Most small businesses in Cameroon do not fail because they hired people. They fail because they hired people without building the systems that keep those people accountable.

The accountability problem

When a business has one owner and no staff, accountability is automatic. The moment you add staff, a gap opens up. You cannot be everywhere. And people behave differently when they know they are not being observed. This is not about distrust. It is about systems.

Setting staff roles and access levels

Not every employee needs access to everything. A cashier needs to record sales. They do not need to see your cost prices, your profit margins, or your financial reports. In ShopTrack, you can assign different access levels to different staff members. Each employee has their own login. What they can see and what they can do is defined by you.

The price lock

One of the most common ways businesses lose money to staff is through below-price selling. ShopTrack allows you to set a minimum selling price for every item. If a staff member tries to sell below that price, the system blocks the transaction automatically. You receive a WhatsApp alert immediately.

The audit trail

ShopTrack logs every action taken in the system with the name of the staff member who performed it, the date, and the time. If a sale was recorded and then deleted, you can see it. This audit trail changes staff behaviour before anything goes wrong.

Real-time sales alerts

The moment any transaction is recorded, you receive a WhatsApp message showing what was sold, the amount, the payment method, and which staff member recorded it. You can be across town and have a live picture of what is happening in your shop.

According to the World Bank SME Finance data, poor internal controls are among the top three reasons small businesses in Sub-Saharan Africa fail to scale. Research from Frontiers in Psychology confirms that transparent monitoring systems significantly reduce workplace misconduct.
