How to Use WhatsApp Business to Grow Your Shop in Cameroon

WhatsApp Business is free, already on most phones in Cameroon, and almost entirely underused by small business owners. Here is exactly how to set it up and use it to attract more customers, send professional catalogues, and sell more every single day.

Most small business owners in Cameroon use WhatsApp every day. But there is a significant difference between using the regular WhatsApp app and using WhatsApp Business — the version designed specifically for businesses. WhatsApp Business is free to download, takes about 20 minutes to set up properly, and gives you tools that the regular app simply does not have: a business profile with your hours and address, a product catalogue, automatic greeting messages, quick reply templates, and the ability to label and organise your customers.

The businesses in Cameroon that use WhatsApp Business properly look more professional, convert more enquiries into sales, and build stronger customer relationships — with almost no additional cost. This guide walks you through everything: setting up your profile, using the catalogue, and combining WhatsApp Business with ShopTrack to automate your customer communication completely.

WhatsApp vs WhatsApp Business — what is the actual difference?

The regular WhatsApp app is a personal messaging tool. WhatsApp Business is a business communication platform built on the same infrastructure. The key differences that matter for a small business in Cameroon are:

  • Business profile: You can display your business name, category, description, address, opening hours, email address, and website link — all visible to anyone who chats with you
  • Product catalogue: You can add your products or services with photos, prices, and descriptions — customers can browse without asking
  • Automatic messages: A greeting message sent automatically to anyone who contacts you for the first time, and an away message sent when you are not available
  • Quick replies: Pre-saved responses to your most common questions — send them with a single tap
  • Labels: Colour-coded tags to organise your conversations — New Customer, Pending Payment, Order Complete, VIP, and so on
  • Broadcast lists: Send a message to up to 256 contacts at once, appearing as a personal message in each person's chat

None of these features exist in the regular WhatsApp app. And none of them cost anything. WhatsApp Business is entirely free.

Setting up your WhatsApp Business profile properly

Most business owners who download WhatsApp Business use it exactly like the regular app — they never fill in their profile, never set up their catalogue, never configure their automatic messages. This wastes almost all of the app's value.

Here is how to set it up correctly:

Step 1 — Download and register

Download WhatsApp Business from the Google Play Store or App Store. Register with a phone number dedicated to your business — ideally your Mobile Money business number. If you only have one number, you can migrate your existing WhatsApp account to WhatsApp Business without losing your chats.

Step 2 — Complete your business profile

Go to Settings → Business Tools → Business Profile. Fill in every field: your business name exactly as you want customers to see it, your business category (shop, salon, photography, etc.), a short description of what you offer, your physical address if you have one, your opening hours, and your website or booking link if you have one. A complete profile makes you look professional and gives customers the information they need before they even message you.

Step 3 — Set up your automatic greeting message

Go to Settings → Business Tools → Greeting Message. Turn it on and write a short, professional welcome message. Something like: "Hello! Thank you for contacting [Your Business Name]. We'll get back to you shortly. In the meantime, check our catalogue to see what we offer." This message sends automatically to anyone who contacts you for the first time, or after 14 days of inactivity.

Step 4 — Set up your away message

Go to Settings → Business Tools → Away Message. Write a message that sends when you are not available: "We're currently closed but we'll respond as soon as we open. Our hours are [your hours]. You can browse our catalogue or book an appointment using the link in our profile." Set it to send outside your business hours. This ensures customers always get a professional response, even at midnight.

Step 5 — Create quick replies for your most common questions

Go to Settings → Business Tools → Quick Replies. Save pre-written answers to questions you receive every day. Common ones for Cameroonian businesses:

  • /price — "Here are our current prices: [link to catalogue or price list]"
  • /hours — "We are open Monday to Saturday, 8am to 6pm"
  • /location — "We are located at [your address]. Here is the map link: [Google Maps link]"
  • /book — "To book an appointment, use this link: [your ShopTrack booking link]"

When a customer asks any of these questions, type the shortcut and it auto-fills the full response. What used to take two minutes of typing takes two seconds.

The WhatsApp Business catalogue — your most powerful sales tool

The catalogue feature in WhatsApp Business lets you create a visual menu of your products or services that any customer can browse directly in WhatsApp — without leaving the app, without visiting a website, without asking you to type out prices. For a small business in Cameroon, this is enormously valuable.

How to add products to your catalogue

Go to Settings → Business Tools → Catalogue → Add new item. For each product or service, add a clear photo, a name, the price, and a short description. Add as many items as you offer. Your catalogue is then accessible through a "View Catalogue" button on your business profile — any customer who visits your WhatsApp profile can browse everything you sell.

How to share your catalogue in chats

In any conversation, tap the attachment icon and select "Catalogue." You can share your entire catalogue or specific items. When a customer asks "what do you sell?" — instead of typing a list or fumbling with photos, you send your catalogue in one tap. The customer sees professional photos, clear prices, and descriptions. This single change converts significantly more enquiries into sales.

Keeping your catalogue up to date

Your catalogue is only useful if it reflects what you actually have. When you add new products, add them to your catalogue. When you change a price, update it. When an item is no longer available, remove it. A customer who sees a price in your catalogue and then hears a different price when they arrive loses trust immediately. Consistency is the foundation of credibility.

If you use ShopTrack to manage your inventory, you can use your ShopTrack catalogue — generated automatically from your inventory — as the source of truth for your WhatsApp Business catalogue. Any price change in ShopTrack should be reflected in your WhatsApp catalogue at the same time.

Using labels to manage your customers professionally

Labels are one of the most practical WhatsApp Business features for small businesses in Cameroon. They let you organise your customer conversations with colour-coded tags so you always know where each customer stands.

Suggested labels for a typical Cameroonian shop:

  • 🔵 New Customer — someone who contacted you for the first time
  • 🟡 Pending Order — customer who has ordered but not yet paid
  • 🟠 Awaiting Payment — customer with outstanding credit
  • 🟢 Order Complete — transaction finished and paid
  • 🔴 VIP — your most loyal, highest-spending customers

With labels, you can filter your conversations instantly — see all customers with outstanding payments, follow up with all pending orders, or send a broadcast to all VIP customers about a new product. This level of organisation, done consistently, makes your business operate more professionally than most competitors.

The broadcast list — your free marketing channel

A broadcast list lets you send one message to up to 256 contacts simultaneously. Each recipient receives it as a personal message — they do not see who else received it. This is completely different from a group, where everyone sees each other. A broadcast feels personal. A group feels like a crowd.

Broadcasts work best for:

  • Announcing new products or services: "We just added [product] — here are the details and price"
  • Seasonal promotions: "This week only, [service] at a reduced price for our existing clients"
  • New stock arrival: "The [product] you asked about is back in stock — limited quantity"
  • Business updates: "We are closed on [date] for [reason]. We reopen on [date]"

One important rule: broadcast recipients must have your number saved in their phone to receive the message. This is why giving every customer your WhatsApp Business number from the first interaction — and making sure they save it — is a long-term investment in your broadcast reach.
